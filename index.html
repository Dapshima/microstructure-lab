Grossman and Miller: the price of immediacy

A seller needs to unload 100 shares today. The natural buyer only arrives tomorrow. Someone risk-averse has to hold the shares overnight through a random shock, and nobody does that for free. Fair value is 100.

Kyle: trading an edge when your own order moves the price

One insider knows the true value. Noise traders send random orders. The market makers see only the total flow and set the price to their best estimate of value given that flow. The insider knows they will, and sizes his order accordingly. Prior fair value is 100.

Insider: x* = (v - μ)/(2λ). Market makers: S = μ + λ(x + u), with λ = Cov(v, flow)/Var(flow). Solving both together gives λ = σ/(2σᵤ) and β = σᵤ/σ. The expected price ends halfway between the prior μ and the truth v.

Glosten and Milgrom: the spread as insurance against informed traders

Every order might come from someone who already knows the answer. The market maker cannot tell who, so she prices each quote at the value she expects given that someone just chose to trade against it. Competition leaves her zero profit; the spread is what covering that risk costs.

Ask half-spread Δₐ = P(informed | buy) × (high value - μ), where P(informed | buy) = αp / (αp + (1-α)/2). The bid half-spread mirrors it with 1-p and μ - low value. Equivalently: ask = E[value | a buy just arrived], bid = E[value | a sell just arrived].

Watch the book learn, trade by trade

Nature picks the true value and hides it. Each period one trader arrives, informed with probability α. After every order the market maker updates her belief with Bayes' rule and re-quotes. Each execution price is her posterior expectation, so the trade prices form a martingale and the quotes converge on the truth.

The prevalence of toxic flow.

Each uninformed trader trades only if the half-spread is below their urgency, drawn uniformly between 0 and the ceiling below.

Ho and Stoll: how far from the mid to rest a quote

A risk-neutral market maker posts a sell order δ ticks above the mid. Post tight and you fill often for a sliver. Post deep and you earn a fat margin on fills that rarely come. Incoming market orders walk into the book a random distance with an exponential tail.

Optimal depth δ* = 1/κ
Fill probability at δ*
Expected profit per arrival at δ*
Your depth δ
Fill probability at your δ
Expected profit per arrival at your δ

Expected profit = p⁺ · e^(-κδ) · δ. Setting the derivative to zero gives δ* = 1/κ, which is also the mean distance an aggressive order walks into the book. At the optimum the fill probability is always 1/e ≈ 36.8%, whatever κ is.

The chance an order walks at least δ ticks deep is e^(-κδ). Large κ: orders rarely walk far, so quote tight.

Scales the profit; it does not change where to post.
